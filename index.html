💡 CW Knowledge Base & Frequently Asked Questions

AEO · Koch Method · Timing · Audio DSP

🎯 What is the Koch Method for Learning Morse Code?

The Koch method, created in 1936 by psychologist Ludwig Koch, is widely recognized as the fastest way to learn Morse code (CW). Instead of starting slow and mentally counting dots and dashes, you train at full target speed (e.g., 20 WPM) from Lesson 1 with just two characters (K and M). You only introduce a new letter after achieving 90%+ copy accuracy, establishing direct auditory reflex recognition.

⏱️ What is Farnsworth Timing in Morse Code?

Farnsworth timing (developed by Donald R. Farnsworth, W6TTB) plays individual characters at high target speed (e.g., 20 WPM character speed) while extending the spacing between letters and words (e.g., 10 WPM overall effective speed). This preserves the sound pattern and rhythm of each character while giving beginners extra thinking time.

🎙️ How Does Real-Time Microphone Audio CW Decoding Work?

9M2PJU Morse Code Lab captures microphone or radio receiver audio via the HTML5 Web Audio API, filters the signal through an adaptive Goertzel single-frequency discrete Fourier transform tuned to your CW pitch (e.g., 700 Hz), applies automatic gain control (AGC) and noise gate thresholding, and classifies pulse durations with an adaptive moving-average timing detector.

📏 What are the Standard Morse Timing Rules (ITU-R M.1677-1)?

Under the international Paris standard (t = 1200 / WPM ms): 1 Dit = 1 unit; 1 Dah = 3 units; element spacing within a letter = 1 unit; inter-character spacing = 3 units; inter-word spacing = 7 units.

🏆 How to Practice Amateur Radio CW Contests?

Use the Contest panel to simulate realistic CQ WW, WPX, and ARRL exchanges. Listen to pileup calls with signal reports (599) and serial numbers or zones, type the callsign and exchange accurately, check instant verification scores, and export your contest QSO log.
